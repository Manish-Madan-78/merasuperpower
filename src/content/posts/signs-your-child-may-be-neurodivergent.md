Every child grows at their own pace, but some children think, learn and experience the world in a different way. These children are neurodivergent: for example, they may have dyslexia, ADHD, autism, dyspraxia or dyscalculia. Spotting the signs early isn't about finding a label. It's about understanding your child so they can get the right support.

Here are five areas to watch.

## 1. Handwriting

Messy handwriting is common in young children. Look out if it stays a struggle well past the age when friends have moved on. Signs include very slow writing, letters that are reversed or unevenly sized, gripping the pencil too hard, or tiredness and frustration after only a few lines. This can point to dysgraphia or dyspraxia. Often the child knows exactly what they want to say but can't get it onto the page.

## 2. Hygiene and self-care

A child who resists brushing teeth, washing hair or changing clothes isn't always being difficult. Some neurodivergent children are very sensitive to touch, sound, smell or texture, so a toothbrush or a shower can feel genuinely unpleasant. Others find multi-step routines hard to plan and remember. Patience and visual routines often help more than repeated reminders.

## 3. Mathematics

Watch for a child who still counts on their fingers long after classmates have stopped, can't remember number facts despite lots of practice, mixes up symbols, or finds it hard to tell time and handle money. This can be a sign of dyscalculia. It has nothing to do with effort or intelligence. Their brain simply processes numbers differently.

## 4. Social behaviour

Some children find it hard to read faces, take turns in conversation, or understand unspoken rules of play. They may prefer to play alone, talk at length about one favourite topic, or get overwhelmed in noisy groups. Others are very impulsive or find it hard to wait. These patterns can be linked to autism or ADHD, and they deserve understanding rather than punishment.

## 5. A surprising strength

This is the sign people most often miss. A child may struggle in several of the areas above and still show an exceptional ability: perfect pitch, a photographic memory, advanced drawing, deep knowledge of a subject, or a striking talent for patterns and puzzles. An uneven profile like this, with real peaks next to real challenges, is very common in neurodivergent children. Children who are highly gifted and also neurodivergent are often called "twice-exceptional". Because the strength can hide the struggle, or the struggle can hide the strength, these children are easy to misunderstand.

## What to do next

One sign on its own means very little. If you see several of these together and they last over time, talk to your child's teacher and a paediatrician or educational psychologist. An assessment can open the door to support at school and at home.

Most of all, remember that a neurodivergent child isn't a broken version of anyone else. With the right understanding, the way they think can become their superpower.
